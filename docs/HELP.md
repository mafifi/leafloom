# Writing with Leafloom

Your library holds books, shelves and pen names. Open a book to write, or choose the **+** on a shelf to start one. Enter a title, then press **Enter** to reach the first chapter. Plotters can start in Outline instead.

## The manuscript

Type on the page. **Enter** makes a paragraph. Press Enter again from an empty paragraph to make a scene break; press it once more to start a chapter. Undo takes you back through those changes.

Use the Format menu for bold, italic, alignment and poetry. In poetry, Shift+Enter continues the verse. Enter returns to prose, carrying any words after the caret into the new paragraph. Shift+Enter in prose begins poetry at the caret. From a chapter heading, Shift+Enter begins an opening verse.

Chapters appear in the pane beside the page. Give a chapter a title, add another, or use its context menu to change its place and role. A book can carry a prologue, parts, an epilogue and the pages that belong around its story.

Leafloom saves as you write. Use **⌘S** on Mac or **Ctrl+S** elsewhere to save immediately. The save indicator tells you when the writing has been saved.

## Notes and Outline

Notes is a separate page for research and working thoughts. Format and paste into it as you would into the manuscript.

Outline describes the book in chapter and section lines. Enter creates another line. Tab turns a fresh chapter into a section of the chapter above; Shift+Tab turns a section into a chapter. Backspace removes an empty line.

A section thought appears as a gray passage in the manuscript. Click it and begin writing to replace the thought with prose. Once you have written over it, later Outline edits preserve those words.

Double-click the Notes or Outline tab to give it a name. That name is also used in future books. Each workspace remembers its scroll position, and the manuscript remembers its caret.

## Darlings

A beautiful passage can belong somewhere else. Select it and use **⌘⇧D** or **Ctrl+Shift+D** to put it in Darlings. Its formatting and original chapter travel with it.

Restore returns a passage to the place it left. If that place has changed beyond recognition, Leafloom puts it at the end of its chapter or the manuscript. Undo restores a cut or a restoration, including the Darling list.

## Finding and replacing

Use **⌘F** or **Ctrl+F** to open Find. A selected passage becomes the starting query. Enter moves to the next match; Shift+Enter moves back. The arrow buttons do the same. Tab returns the caret to the end of the current match so you can keep writing.

Replace changes one match. All changes matching passages throughout the manuscript in one operation. Undo reverses that operation.

## Goals and sprints

Click the daily counter, or use **⌘,** or **Ctrl+,**, to open Goals and Progress. Set a daily target, a book target and the hour your writing day ends. The chart shows thirty days of writing and the book's total.

Start a sprint with a word target. Its counter measures new words from that point. End it early to see the words written and elapsed minutes, or keep writing until the completion notice appears.

Select a passage to see its word count. Click the word counter to change between the book and the current chapter. The story's words count toward goals; the surrounding publication pages do not.

## Shelves and pen names

Use the author name in the library to choose a writing identity. Each identity has its own shelves. Rename a shelf by editing its label; press Enter to finish. Drag its grip to change the shelf order.

A book's cover is painted from its saved seed, with the title and author set as real type. Choose **New cover** from the book's context menu to give it another painting.

## Sharing a draft

Use Export to choose a manuscript format: text, Markdown, HTML, Word, EPUB or PDF. The book's publication pages and chapter order travel with the story.
